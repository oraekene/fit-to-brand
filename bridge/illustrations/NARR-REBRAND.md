# Narrative 2 — The amber rescue: rebranding SunJar without burning it down

*Companion to `ILLU-REBRAND.md` and `data-rebrand/`. Two years after Narrative 1.
Same storytelling contract: fictional people and figures, real mechanics.*

For two years the amber rail worked. Then the confusion log started filling — fourteen
shoppers in a hundred reaching for SunJar and lifting LuminaHome, both blue-adjacent
at three meters under dealer fluorescents — and the pricing murmurs turned into a
pattern: the matte charcoal read budget against a mid PAYG price. The system wasn't
broken. It was misread, at exactly the distance that mattered, by exactly the buyers
who mattered.

The rebrand began with a refusal. The team wanted to start over — new name energy,
blank Figma files, the relief of demolition. The orchestration said no: before any
diagnosis, write the equity guardrails. One approved page. Must-keep: the amber rail,
the trial promise, the wall-rail pack zone. Must-drop: anything reading blue at three
meters. And a kill trigger aimed at the rebrand itself: hold launch if new-vs-old
recall falls below sixty percent. Only with that page signed did anyone open the v1
files. Beginning with what you will not destroy is the difference between a rebrand
and a fire.

The spec freeze that followed was an exercise in honesty about the present: SunJar
as it shipped, blue-adjacent labels and all, with the old pack graphics listed in
Out-of-Scope as deprecated. The theme verdict came back evolve — the broad promise
held, the sharp edge moved from the business model to the visual signature, from
"paid light" to "amber-rail light." The differentiator had migrated from how
customers paid to how they recognized. That sentence, once written, made half the
subsequent decisions obvious.

Nobody re-enumerated the market; the moments hadn't moved. One delta pass re-scored
the school-lighting situation, which had grown from a footnote into three term
contracts. Then the catalog did its cold work: SunJar v1 entered as an incumbent
among incumbents, with the finding stated plainly — blue-adjacent fields confuse at
three meters; charcoal under-signals the tier by a band. The fit pass obeyed its one
sacred rule: the brand's rows against itself shipped as component-or-retire, never
swap. You cannot disrupt yourself; you can only succeed yourself.

Regrouping was surgery. Households kept, brief rewritten to say the thing out loud:
"the amber hub, not the blue box — ask for the amber rail by name." Traders kept,
brief carried over. The agent group retired by note, folded into dealer ops, its row
kept so the counts would stay honest — deletion would have been tidier and a lie.
And from the school evidence, a split: committees, termly payment, certified fitting,
a brand-new brief with its own prediction row.

Then the audit of the living brand — Stage 3's instrument turned on the patient, not
a draft. One direction, yes. Competing languages, yes: rail versus label, amber
versus blue-adjacent, the confusion visible at last as craft, not fate. Pack
recognizable at three meters: no. Verdict: REJECT AND RESTART, on foundational
grounds — color logic, pack architecture, tier fit. The mechanics were merciless and
fair: v1 marked never-to-be-used-as-reference, only abstract keeps surviving, no
blending of old and new. But v1 stayed in the room as the migration baseline, because
you cannot photograph the distance traveled without the starting point.

What followed was delta work, not drama: touched guideline modules regenerated with
old-vs-new misapplications as Don'ts; the key visual revalidated; migration pairs —
same bedroom, old blue, new amber — built for every kept group, because pairs, not
singles, are the unit of launch review. Brand Lock barred every old-look element by
name. The test pair passed audit. Scale proceeded across carried and new threads.

The probes decided the launch, as designed: seventy-one percent recall against a
sixty gate, confusion down to six from fourteen. The school wedge held — three term
plans in twenty visits. Findings went to their owners, the old packs sold through
their eight-week window, and the v1 Anchor was archived SUPERSEDED: kept for
reference, never again a generation input, like a retired instrument in a glass case.

Adaeze's printout this time has fewer lines and harder ones. The rebrand didn't save
SunJar. The guardrails did — by deciding, before the exciting part, what surviving
looked like.
