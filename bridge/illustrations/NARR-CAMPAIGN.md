# Narrative 3 — Forty assets, one brand: the harmattan season

*Companion to `ILLU-CAMPAIGN.md` and `data-campaign/`. The amber system is live and
scaling. Marketing wants a season's worth of assets. Fictional people and figures, as
always; real mechanics throughout.*

The request arrived like they always do: fast, bundled, and slightly illegal. Four
wants — a harmattan-season variant for households, a vertical stall film, a
Yoruba-language market-day edition, a rooftop web cut — plus, tucked inside the
Yoruba brief, a brand-new headline nobody in strategy had ever approved, and, from
sales, a casual "add a cooling claim for the shops." A year earlier, all of it would
have been made. That was the problem the campaign orchestration existed to solve.

Entry gates first. Anchor approved: yes, on the wall. Lock roles confirmed and a
passing test asset: yes for the bedroom family — which meant the rooftop repeat
could move fast and the harmattan variant, a new scene family, would need its own
test asset before scaling a single frame. Predictions: three rows, each with a kill
line and a reopen line, or nothing flies. The invented Yoruba headline died here,
politely and in writing: no brief sources it, so the edition would carry the GRP-02
brief verbatim through the approved translation mapping, nothing more. The cooling
claim died one stage later with an address attached — back to S5A, where S-0004
already says X. Refusals with addresses are not friction. They are the product.

The thread plan sequenced the work the way money flows: proven rooftop first, to fund
the learning, then the vertical, then harmattan — one new scene family at a time, so
a failure would have a name. The rooftop loop was almost boring, and the narrative
insists on the boring part, because boring is what scale feels like when the system
holds: roles confirmed, plan approved, no new test asset needed, audit APPROVED,
three SKU cuts, filed by scene and format with cost actuals attached.

Harmattan was the slow path and the honest one. New dust, new light, new family —
fresh test asset, and the audit caught it: the haze had washed the amber rail below
contrast spec. CORRECTION REQUIRED, major, with the preserve list holding everything
else steady. One correction — the rail grade lifted, nothing else touched — one
re-audit, APPROVED, then scale. Somewhere in those two audit lines lives the answer
to how forty assets come to feel like one brand: nobody skipped the loop when they
were tired. Especially when they were tired.

The lateral-additions table earned its place mid-season. The vertical needed a true
recompose, not a crop, plus safe-area and hierarchy checks — and because the
composition changed materially, its own test asset and audit. The Yoruba edition
moved under mapping-only rules with a legal flag on the PAYG phrasing. Each bonus
ran in order — responsive, localization, banner, motion last, from locked stills —
each audited before joining the scaled set.

Then the market graded the work, per thread, without averaging. Harmattan converted:
SCALE, second household scene family approved. The vertical swiped beautifully and
converted nothing — leading up, lagging flat — and the decision log shows the
discipline that matters most in the whole loop: FIX-LOGIC, not a redesign. The
expression worked; the placement or the call to action didn't. Thumb reach, first
frame, hook placement — reopen the brief's delivery, not the kit. And the Yoruba
print stock read cooler than the screen, pushing confusion with the rival to twelve
percent: FIX-BOUNDARY, strengthen the prohibition, five-person re-probe before
reflight. Three verdicts, three different addresses, zero redesigns of things that
weren't broken.

The season closed with the findings filed where they belong: a format-placement note
in the traders' brief, a print-stock boundary in NOT-fit, the type-finishing fallback
revalidated for vertical safe areas. Kill latency — six days from trip to fix on the
vertical — logged as the loop's own report card.

Nobody at SunJar remembers the harmattan season as the time they made forty assets.
They remember it as the time the new headline died in the gate review, the cooling
claim got an address instead of an argument, and the vertical got fixed instead of
redesigned. The assets were the exhaust. The decisions were the product.
